TL;DR

Complexity-Balanced Splitting (CBS) is a principled framework that replaces monolithic diffusion architectures by distributing the generative workload across specialized sub-networks. By mathematically partitioning the diffusion timeline based on local approximation burden (e.g., path acceleration), CBS significantly improves image synthesis quality (up to ~35% FID improvement) without increasing per-step inference costs.

Abstract

Standard continuous-time generative models rely on monolithic architectures that must navigate vastly different signal regimes, from isotropic noise to intricate data distributions. While scaling model capacity improves performance, deploying a massive network uniformly across the entire generative timeline is inherently inefficient. In this work, we propose Complexity-Balanced Splitting (CBS), a principled framework for temporal capacity allocation that distributes the generative workload across multiple specialized sub-networks. Grounded in function approximation theory and de Boor's equidistribution principle, CBS partitions the diffusion timeline into segments of equal approximation burden, allocating more representational capacity to regions where the generative dynamics are more difficult to model. To estimate this local complexity, we introduce two complementary and tractable monitor functions: a spatial measure based on the flow's Dirichlet energy, and a geometric measure based on the acceleration of the sampling trajectories. Using a lightweight auxiliary model to estimate these complexity profiles, our approach eliminates the need for heuristic temporal splits or computationally expensive search procedures. Extensive evaluation across multiple architectures (SiT, JiT, and UNet) and datasets demonstrates that CBS consistently improves synthesis quality without increasing per-step inference cost. In particular, CBS improves FID by ~35% on SiT-XL with CFG relative to naive temporal partitioning.

How CBS Works

Deploying a single massive network uniformly across the entire generative timeline is inefficient. The generative process shifts from broad structural formation to high-frequency detail refinement. CBS dynamically allocates capacity precisely where the generative dynamics are hardest to model.

1. Equidistribution

Grounded in de Boor's Equidistribution Principle, we treat timeline partitioning as a domain decomposition problem. CBS divides the diffusion timeline into segments of equal approximation burden, ensuring every sub-network faces a comparable workload.

2. Tractable Monitors

To measure this local approximation burden, we introduce two robust monitor functions:

  • Dirichlet Energy: A spatial measure linking rapid flow variations to frequency complexity.
  • Path Acceleration: A geometric measure capturing the "curviness" of the sampling trajectories.

3. Search-Free Splitting

By pre-computing the cumulative complexity using a highly lightweight auxiliary model (e.g., trained for just 50K iterations on 10% of the data), CBS yields the optimal boundaries automatically—eliminating the need for computationally expensive heuristic searches.
